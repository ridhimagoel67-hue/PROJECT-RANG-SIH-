# Project RANG — Banana Fibre & Pulp for Sustainable Fashion


Smart India Hackathon 2026 submission — turning banana pseudo-stem waste into hand-painted, sustainable fabric and zero-waste biodegradable packaging, with 60–70% of the profit margin going directly to rural women artisans.


## 1. Project Information

- **Problem Statement ID:** SIH26214

- **Team Name:** RANG

## 2. Problem Statement

Three gaps motivate this project:

- **Artisan marginalization** — rural women have real craft skills but no access to high-end, modern global markets.
- **The market gap** — conscious consumers are stuck between overpriced sustainable luxury and low-quality mass production, with no affordable ethical middle ground.
- **Environmental cost** — traditional cotton is water-intensive, and the fashion industry needs a genuinely sustainable raw material alternative.

## 3. Proposed Solution

RANG builds a circular, banana-based value chain with three pillars:

- **Economic empowerment (the "Who"):** upskilling homemakers in design and financial literacy, turning latent talent into a steady, direct income stream.
- **Affordable sustainability (the "Why"):** eco-friendly, hand-painted, paint-to-order products at competitive prices — making ethical fashion accessible, not a luxury.
- **Direct market linkage (the "How"):** a Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) model that eliminates middlemen, so 60–70% of the profit margin goes straight back to the women artisans.

**Rollout plan**

| Stage | Focus |
|---|---|
| Initial | Outsourced sustainable banana-fibre fabric, low-risk fast market entry, paint-to-order (low waste), fair & direct payments, story tag with every product |
| Later | In-house circular banana-based system — banana pulp converted into plastic-free, low-cost, biodegradable packaging and tags |

## 4. Technical Approach

One banana pseudo-stem yields three streams:

| Component | Share | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Central core | 10–15% | Other industries (candy, pickle) |
| Banana fibre | 1.5–2% | Banana fabric for tote bags, bedsheets, etc. |
| Waste pulp | 85–90% | Biodegradable packaging |

**Process:** pseudo-stem → cut/crushed (using low-cost, readily available sugarcane crushers) → fibre extracted, washed, dried, spun into yarn → yarn woven into banana-fibre fabric. The leftover pulp (previously discarded) is boiled/softened and processed into molded, biodegradable packaging trays and boxes instead of plastic.

See [docs/technical-approach.md](docs/technical-approach.md) for the full pipeline and diagrams.

## 5. Feasibility & Viability

- **Technical:** banana pulp packaging is reported ~3x stronger than wood pulp with better tear resistance.
- **Operational:** fully circular — leftover pulp from textile extraction feeds directly into packaging molds.
- **Economic:** banana pulp is cheaper to produce than plastic or paper packaging.
- **Raw material supply:** India generates an estimated ~30M tons of pseudo-stem waste annually — a near-free input.
- **Proven model:** comparable banana-fibre businesses (e.g., Musa Pacta) already process 12–13 tons/month profitably.

Key risks and mitigations are documented in [docs/feasibility-and-risk.md](docs/feasibility-and-risk.md).

## 6. Impact & Benefits

| Dimension | Highlights |
|---|---|
| Environmental | Banana fabric, water-based/natural dyes, paint-to-order (near-zero waste), minimal packaging from banana pulp |
| Social | Women-led home-based work, flexible hours, skill ownership (not dependency), fair & transparent payments |
| Economic | Revenue-generating (not donation-dependent) model, repeat-use products, profit reinvested for scale |

**Before → After for artisans:** from informal, middlemen-dependent, low-confidence work to skill-certified, directly-paid entrepreneurs earning a stable ₹8,500–₹9,000/month over 18–22 working days, with full pricing independence.

**Target outcomes:** 200% income growth · 100% women paid directly · 0 middlemen involvement · 100% skill certification.
