# Feasibility, Viability, Scalability & Risk

## Feasibility

- **Technical:** Banana pulp produces packaging reported to be roughly 3x stronger than wood pulp, with superior tear resistance.
- **Operational:** 100% circular model — leftover pulp from textile fibre extraction feeds directly into packaging molds, so there is no separate raw-material sourcing step for packaging.
- **Economic:** Banana pulp is significantly more cost-effective to produce than traditional plastic or paper packaging.

## Viability

- **Vast raw material base:** India generates an estimated ~30 million tons of pseudo-stem waste annually, providing a near-free raw material supply.
- **Proven model:** Comparable banana-fibre businesses (e.g., Musa Pacta) already process 12–13 tons of material per month profitably.
- **B2B expansion potential:** There is significant potential to sell low-cost banana pulp packaging directly to other businesses, independent of the core fashion product line.

## Scalability

- **Integrated craft hubs:** Set up dual production wings — one for textiles, one dedicated to pulp-molded packaging.
- **In-house pulp lab:** Phase out third-party pulp sourcing over time to produce 100% of packaging in-house.
- **Bio-plastics R&D:** Develop banana-peel starch into compostable, transparent protective sleeves as a next-generation packaging layer.

## Risk vs. Solution

| # | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | High setup cost for pulp-lab machinery | Outsource pulp processing in the initial stage; reinvest early profits to build in-house capacity over time |
| 2 | B2B buyers doubt bio-packaging can match plastic's strength | Lead marketing with certified data showing packaging is "3x stronger than wood pulp" |
| 3 | Inconsistent raw pseudo-stem collection from farms | Partner with established NGO/rural supply networks (e.g., Goonj-style networks) for steady, reliable stem collection |
| 4 | Production waste management | Zero-waste lifecycle design — all stem remnants and fabric offcuts are repurposed into packaging, leaving no residual waste stream |

## Other Known Challenges

- **Initial setup cost:** High capital required to eventually build an in-house pulp-molding lab.
- **Durability doubts:** Convincing B2B buyers that bio-packaging matches plastic's strength (addressed via certified test data, see Risk #2).
- **Transport logistics:** Efficiently transporting heavy, wet pseudo-stems from farms to processing units.
