# Technical Approach

## One banana stem → fabric + zero-waste packaging

A single banana pseudo-stem splits into three usable streams:

- **Central core (10–15%)** → usable by other industries (candy, pickle).
- **Banana fibre (1.5–2%)** → used to make banana fibre fabric for tote bags, bedsheets, etc.
- **Waste material / fabric pulp (85–90%)** → used to make biodegradable packaging.

## Fibre-to-fabric process

1. Banana pseudo-stem is harvested and cut.
2. The pseudo-stem is fed into a crusher — sugarcane crushers are used because they are cheaper and readily available compared to specialized pulping machinery.
3. Fibre is extracted from the crushed stem.
4. Fibre is dried, and sap/waste pulp is separated out.
5. Dried fibre is spun into yarn.
6. Yarn is woven into banana fibre fabric.
7. Finished banana fibre products (bags, fabric rolls, etc.) are produced.

## Pulp-to-packaging process

1. Leftover pulp — previously discarded as waste — is boiled and softened.
2. Softened pulp is processed further (pulp processing stage).
3. Processed fabric pulp is molded into packaging shapes (trays, boxes).
4. The result is biodegradable, plastic-free packaging that also carries the product's story tag.

## Why this matters

- Nothing from the pseudo-stem is wasted — the process is fully circular.
- The core packaging input (waste pulp) is essentially a byproduct of the fibre-extraction process, keeping raw material costs low.
- Packaging is plastic-free and biodegradable by design, replacing conventional plastic and paper packaging.
